1. Welcome

The Islamic Business Funding Navigator helps SMEs, founders and investment consultants answer four practical questions: Where does the business really stand? Which Islamic financing structure fits? What will it cost and who carries which risk? How do we run it so the business is sustainable and serves real customer needs?

It compares Murabaha (cost-plus sale), Ijarah (lease), Musharakah (equity partnership, in its diminishing form) and Mudarabah (trust-based profit partnership). It follows the Creed-to-Commerce™ journey so the creed leads and commerce follows.

Who it is for

  • SME owners deciding how to fund equipment, stock, premises or growth.
  • Founders of early ventures who need to understand what a financier or partner will expect.
  • Investment and business consultants preparing client cases, board papers and funder conversations.
  • Capital providers who want to see how the structures share risk before proposing terms.
What this tool is, and is notIt produces indicative, educational estimates from your inputs and editable assumptions. It is not a quote, a fatwa, or Shariah, legal, tax or investment advice. Every real contract needs review by a qualified Shariah scholar or board and a licensed professional.

2. Quick start (10 minutes)

  1. Try the sample first. Click Load Sample Data in the top bar. A fictional bakery case fills every screen and a gold banner reminds you it is sample data.
  2. Walk the five stages using the tabs under the header: Creed, Clarity, Compare, Contract, Commerce. Notice how the charts and rankings respond.
  3. Download a report. Click Download PDF Report. The PDF carries a SAMPLE DATA watermark.
  4. Clear the sample. Click Remove Sample Data and confirm. All fields return to blank.
  5. Enter a real case. Start at Creed, complete Clarity, then read Compare, Contract and Commerce in order.
  6. Create your documents. Open the Reports tab and download the Findings Report, Operations Brief or Client Snapshot.
TipFill in what you know and leave the rest blank. The app never blocks you. Blank answers lower the confidence of the recommendation rather than stopping the analysis.

4. Stage 1: Creed

Every Islamic financing structure rests on a permissible activity and honest dealing. This stage puts intention first.

The Creed screen

Tick each statement only if it is true today:

  • Core activity is halal. The products, services and customers are permissible. No structure can make an impermissible activity permissible. If this is unticked, the readiness score is reduced and the app reminds you to resolve it first.
  • No reliance on interest-based products. The project will not depend on interest-bearing loans or similar dealings.
  • Truthful accounts. Records and projections given to funders and partners are accurate and complete.
  • Terms are clear before signing. Price, term, ownership and obligations will be understood and written before money moves.
  • Zakat and charity are planned for. Includes zakat on eligible assets and a charity route for late-payment charges.

A status chip shows how many of the five are confirmed. These count for 15 of the 100 readiness points.

Who and why

Choose your role, enter the business or client name, who prepared the analysis, and the currency. Then write a purpose statement: what good the funding will do and for whom. The name, preparer and purpose appear on your PDFs. The currency changes the symbols used across the app (PDFs use the three-letter code, such as USD or MYR).

6. Stage 3: Compare

Compare runs the same financed amount, term and project return through all four structures so you see like with like. It needs a project cost and term from Clarity; the risk matrix is available even before.

What you see

  • Summary tiles: amount financed, term, project return assumed and your monthly cash-flow base.
  • Comparison table: payment nature (fixed or moves with results), average monthly outflow, peak month, total expected cost, effective annual cost and DSCR for each structure.
  • "What happens if results change?" chart and table: financing cost and the business's net result under expected, downside and stress outcomes.
  • Risk matrix: for seven risk areas it shows whether the Financier, the Business or both (Shared) carry the risk.

How the numbers are calculated

Amount financed (F) = Total project cost − your own contribution. Term in years = months ÷ 12. An arrangement fee (% of F) is added to every structure.
Murabaha: total markup = F × markup rate × years (flat). Monthly instalment = (F + markup) ÷ months. Price is fixed for the whole term.
Ijarah: monthly rental is a declining-balance (annuity) payment on F at the rental rate, plus a monthly takaful charge of F × takaful % ÷ 12. Total cost = total rentals − F + takaful.
Musharakah (diminishing): each month the business buys back F ÷ months of the financier's units. The financier's profit share each month = outstanding units × project return ÷ 12. Payments flex as results change; in a loss the financier absorbs its capital share.
Mudarabah: total profit on funded capital = F × return × years. The capital provider receives its agreed share, paid monthly, and capital is returned at the end (reduced by any genuine loss). In a loss year the capital provider carries the financial loss and the business loses its effort.
Effective annual cost converts each structure's real monthly cash flows into a declining-balance annual rate so a flat Murabaha markup can be compared fairly with an Ijarah rental or a profit share. A flat 8% Murabaha markup over four years is roughly 16% effective.
DSCR (debt service coverage) = monthly operating cash flow ÷ (existing monthly debt + this structure's average monthly outflow). Aim for 1.3x or higher; below 1.1x is weak.
Business net result = project profit on the financed capital − cost of financing. For Mudarabah in a loss scenario, only the arrangement fee is lost because the capital provider bears the financial loss.

Scenarios. Expected uses your project return. Downside uses that return multiplied by the downside factor (default 40%). Stress uses a fixed return (default −10% a year). Fixed-payment structures charge the same in every scenario, which is exactly the risk-sharing difference this chart is built to reveal.

7. Stage 4: Contract

This stage answers "which structure suits us?" and "how does it actually operate?"

The recommendation

The top card names the best-fit structure, a confidence level and the main reasons. Below it, four ranked cards show each structure's fit score (0–100), its strongest reasons and its main caution. Click a card, or use the buttons beneath, to study any structure in detail. Your choice is remembered and also drives the Commerce stage and the Operations Brief PDF.

Hybrid flagWhen the top two scores are within a few points, the app suggests considering a hybrid. Many businesses finance a tangible asset with Murabaha or Ijarah and fund growth or working capital with a partnership structure.

How best fit is chosen

Each structure starts from a base score and gains or loses points from your answers, for example:

  • Murabaha gains from asset or stock purchases, an identifiable asset, a preference for fixed payments, a wish to keep full control and comfortable cash-flow cover. It loses for pure working capital, thin cash flow and early-stage businesses.
  • Ijarah gains from leasable assets (equipment, vehicles, premises), low upfront outlay and comfortable cover. It loses for consumable stock and working capital.
  • Musharakah gains from growth or project purposes, strong records, openness to sharing ownership, an established track record and thin fixed-payment capacity. It loses sharply for weak records or a wish to keep full control.
  • Mudarabah gains for new ventures, trusted track records, strong records, experienced managers and an identified capital provider. It loses with weak records, no capital provider or a need for certain payments.

Raw points are scaled to a 0–100 fit score. Confidence reflects data quality: High when at least 85% of nine key inputs are filled and the top structure leads by 10+ points, Moderate at 65% or more, otherwise Exploratory. The nine inputs are: use of funds, stage, identifiable asset, willingness to share, fixed-payment preference, capital partner, records quality, project cost and term.

Operational details for the selected structure

  • Key numbers for your case: average monthly outflow, total cost, effective annual cost and cash-flow cover.
  • Step-by-step operation from first request to closing out the contract.
  • Documents to prepare, the Shariah conditions to confirm, each side's obligations and red flags to watch for.
  • Accounting treatment so finance staff and auditors know what to expect.

8. Stage 5: Commerce

A financing structure only works if the business is healthy enough to carry it and customers keep needing what it sells. This stage turns the choice into a sustainable pathway for the structure you selected in Contract.

Sustainability indicators

IndicatorMeaning and guide
Average monthly outflowWhat the structure costs you each month, including capital repayment or unit buy-out.
Cash-flow cover (DSCR)1.3x or higher is healthy; 1.1–1.3x is tight; below 1.1x is weak.
Payments as % of revenueA 15% guide: annual outflow ÷ annual revenue.
Reserve coverCash reserves ÷ monthly outflow. Aim for three months or more.
Return needed to break evenFor fixed structures, the project return needed on financed capital just to cover cost. Partnership structures show "None fixed" because payments flex.
Effective annual costDeclining-balance equivalent in the expected case.
Customer-need alignmentAverage of demand evidence, repeat-customer share (80% repeat = full marks) and whether you described the customer need.

Stress test

Shows financing cost and your net result under expected, downside and stress returns for the selected structure. Use it to ask: could we survive the stress case, and who would carry the loss?

Three-horizon roadmap

  • Next 90 days: Clarity and preparation. Actions drawn from your weakest readiness components, plus obtaining quotes and Shariah review.
  • 3–12 months: Execute the contract well. Implementation steps specific to the selected structure, a customer-service promise to track and a payment reserve target.
  • 12–36 months: Sustain and grow. Annual re-run, customer diversification, zakat and charity routines and an end-of-term plan.

Each action has a checkbox. Ticks are saved in your browser, so you can use the roadmap as a living checklist.

9. PDF reports

All reports are created in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. Files download with a name that includes the report type and the client name.

Funding Findings Report

The full document: executive summary, Creed screen, business status and readiness, cost comparison, scenarios, risk matrix, fit ranking, the recommended structure's operating steps and documents, and the sustainability roadmap. Typically 4 pages. Also available from the top-bar Download PDF Report button.

Structure Operations Brief

A working guide for the structure currently selected in Contract: how it works, risk allocation, obligations, a tick-box documents checklist, Shariah conditions, red flags and accounting treatment. Good for briefing a team or discussing terms with a provider.

One-Page Client Snapshot

The ask, readiness status, recommended structure, headline economics, reasons and the first three actions on a single page. Ideal for a client, board or capital partner who will not read the full report.

Print view

The Print button prints every stage in sequence with navigation hidden. It also serves as a backup route to a PDF.

Before you send a reportCheck that the SAMPLE DATA watermark is absent (use Remove Sample Data first), that the client name and preparer are filled in, and that the planning assumptions have been replaced with real quotes where you have them.

10. Workflows by role

SME owner or founder

  1. Complete the Creed screen honestly and write your purpose statement.
  2. Enter your real numbers in Clarity. Fix the priority gaps if the score is below 55.
  3. Study Compare and the stress test. Ask: can I carry the fixed payments if sales fall?
  4. Read the operating steps and red flags for your top two structures in Contract.
  5. Take the Operations Brief to providers or partners and ask for quotes. Enter them as assumptions and re-run.
  6. Use the Commerce roadmap as your monthly checklist after funding.

Investment or business consultant

  1. Open a client session, enter the client name and your name, then gather the Clarity inputs in an interview.
  2. Use the risk matrix to explain, in plain language, who carries which risk under each structure.
  3. Adjust assumptions live with the client to show how pricing changes affect outflow and effective cost.
  4. Deliver the Findings Report and the one-page Snapshot. Use Export inputs (JSON) on the Reports tab to keep a case file, then Remove Sample Data to clear the screen before the next client.
  5. To reopen a case later, use Import inputs on the Reports tab.
ConfidentialityEntries stay in the browser of the device you are using. On a shared computer, export what you need, then clear the form before leaving.

Capital provider

Use Compare and the risk matrix to see how your exposure changes between a fixed-return sale or lease and a profit-and-loss partnership, and use the Operations Brief to prepare the right documents and conditions.

11. Saving, privacy and sample data

  • Automatic saving. Every change is saved in your browser's local storage on that device. Closing and reopening the page keeps your work.
  • No uploads. There is no server, account or database. Nothing leaves your device.
  • Different browser or device = empty app. Use Export inputs (JSON) to move a case, and Import inputs to load it elsewhere.
  • Private or cleared browsing can erase saved data. Export anything important.
  • Remove Sample Data wipes everything, not just sample values. Export first if you want to keep a case.

13. Deploy with GitHub and Vercel

The app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step.

Files

  • index.html: the app
  • manual.html: this manual
  • vendor/jspdf.umd.min.js: the PDF library (keep this folder beside index.html)
  • vercel.json, README.md, .gitignore

Steps

  1. Create a new empty repository on GitHub, for example islamic-funding-navigator.
  2. In the project folder run: git init, git add ., git commit -m "Funding Navigator", git branch -M main.
  3. Add your repository: git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-USER/islamic-funding-navigator.git then git push -u origin main.
  4. In Vercel choose Add New → Project and import the repository.
  5. Set Framework Preset: Other. Leave build command and output directory empty. Click Deploy.
  6. Your app is live at the Vercel address; the manual is at /manual. Every future push to main redeploys automatically.
Custom domainIn your Vercel project open Settings → Domains to attach your own domain or subdomain.

14. Customising

  • Default pricing: edit DEF_ASSUMP near the top of the script in index.html.
  • Structure content (steps, documents, conditions, red flags): edit the MODELS object.
  • Fit-scoring rules: edit the fit() function. Each rule is a condition, a point change and a plain-language reason.
  • Readiness weights: edit the readiness() function.
  • Colours and fonts: change the variables at the top of the style block (--green, --gold, --brown, fonts).
  • Sectors list: edit the Sector drop-down in the Clarity section.
Have changes reviewedIf you change structure content or the Shariah conditions, have a qualified scholar review the new wording before you share or sell the app.

15. Troubleshooting and FAQ

The comparison table is empty.
Enter a total project cost and a term (in months) in Clarity. The risk matrix works without them.
The recommendation says "Exploratory".
Several key inputs are blank. Complete use of funds, stage, asset, willingness to share, payment preference, capital partner and records quality.
The PDF button says the library did not load.
Make sure the vendor folder with jspdf.umd.min.js sits next to index.html (and was deployed). As a fallback, use Print and choose "Save as PDF".
My data vanished.
Data lives in one browser on one device. A different browser, a private window or cleared site data starts blank. Use Export and Import.
The sample banner is still showing after I changed the numbers.
That is by design. Editing sample values does not make them real. Use Remove Sample Data, then enter your own.
Why is effective cost so much higher than the quoted rate?
A flat rate is charged on the original amount for the whole term, while the balance falls as you repay. The effective rate corrects for that so structures can be compared fairly.
Why does Musharakah show a high effective cost?
Its cost reflects the project's actual profit. With an 18% expected return, the financier's profit share is high in the expected case. In weaker cases it falls or turns into a shared loss.
Why does Mudarabah show a huge peak month?
Capital is returned in full at the end of the term. Plan a reserve or negotiate staged capital return.
Does the app give a fatwa or approve a contract?
No. It is an educational planning aid. Shariah compliance is decided by a qualified scholar or Shariah board on the actual contract.
Does the arrangement fee belong in a Murabaha?
Real providers differ on fees and what they cover. Treat the fee as a placeholder and check the actual fee schedule.

16. Glossary

Gharar
Excessive uncertainty in a contract. Terms must be clear before signing.
Ibra'
A rebate or waiver the financier may give, for example on early settlement.
Ijarah / Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek
Lease / lease ending in transfer of ownership through a separate undertaking.
Mudarabah, Mudarib, Rabb-ul-mal
Profit-sharing partnership; the managing party; the capital provider.
Murabaha
A sale where the seller discloses cost and adds an agreed profit.
Musharakah / Diminishing Musharakah
Partnership where all parties contribute capital; in the diminishing form the business buys out the financier over time.
Riba
Interest or an unjustified increase on a loan; avoided in all four structures.
Takaful
Cooperative, Shariah-compliant insurance.
Tawarruq
A cash-raising arrangement using commodity sales; scholars differ on its use, so seek board approval.
Wa'd
A promise, such as a promise to purchase or lease.
Zakat
Obligatory annual charity on eligible wealth, including certain business assets.
DSCR
Debt service coverage ratio: cash available for payments divided by the payments due.
Effective annual cost
A declining-balance annual rate that makes different structures comparable.

17. Important notice

The Islamic Business Funding Navigator is an educational planning tool. Its calculations are indicative, use assumptions you can edit and are not quotes. It is not a fatwa, and it does not provide Shariah, legal, tax, accounting or investment advice. Rulings differ between scholars, schools and jurisdictions. Before signing any financing or partnership contract, have it reviewed by a qualified Shariah scholar or board and by a licensed legal and financial professional.
